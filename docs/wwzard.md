# Wwzard at work

Open `wwzard.html` for the living illustration, `demos.html#wwzard-desk` for the gallery, or `/?demo=wwzard-desk` for the editable Studio scene. This is local implementation work; it has not been deployed by this task.

The character follows the [user-supplied reference](references/wwzard-style-reference.png). Editable path artwork and authored linear gradients live in `examples/wwzard-artwork.js`. The head, torso, hat, sleeves and hands have joints. The folded tip deforms with the crown instead of rotating as a separate flap. `examples/wwzard-illustration.js` supplies the active work, response, rest, close, closed-idle, closed-pause, and open clips with mood variants and input states. The existing older wwwzard artwork remains separately labeled in Studio.

Furniture and character parts share the core scene-depth ordering: complete robe and its shading at 0, near upper arm at 2, desk at 5, keyboard at 10, far forearm at 20, face/hat at 30, near forearm at 40, and laptop lid at 50. The robe extends behind the furniture instead of ending along the desk's edge. The desk, computer and character belong to the same scene layer so their depths can interleave; the ground shadow stays in the background. An opaque desk silhouette under its adjacent shaded faces prevents antialias seams from showing the robe through. These are saved scene-depth values and normal artwork, with no Wwzard renderer exceptions. A renderer-output regression checks ordering through the saved clips after JSON reopening.

Each sleeve uses the shared `depthSplit` contour contract. The far shoulder stays at -10 behind the robe; the near shoulder stays at 2 in front of the robe and behind the furniture. Only the forearm regions follow the animated depths. Giving both shoulders the far arm's order hid the near upper sleeve and exposed a diagonal torso facet, so their order is deliberately asymmetric. Both regions retain one full drawing, gradient and morph target, and sleeve shading inherits the same split. Studio exposes the split position and both depth anchors. Pixel checks prove far-shoulder coverage and near-shoulder visibility across typing, rest, curiosity and greeting in both SVG and Canvas, in addition to the desk and hand checks.

## Live behavior

`examples/wwzard-behavior.js` builds a normal saved behavior graph. The host sends `visitor` to request attention, `quiet` to clear pending attention, `close-laptop` to close the lid, or `open-laptop` to reopen it. The same events drive the page buttons and gallery controls; clicking the character uses its saved pointer binding. The older `laptop` event still requests a close for existing integrations.

The Disappointed, Normal, and Angry buttons send saved `mood-disappointed`, `mood-normal`, and `mood-angry` events. The graph stores mood as 0, 1, or 2, with Normal as the default. Every activity selects its matching editable clip when it starts, so a mood change finishes the current movement before it appears. The wizard and laptop screen select matching mood clips for closing, closed idle, and opening; the quiet closed pause holds the completed idle pose. The page's motion study also offers an authored `closed-pause` clip and shows the selected mood at the same normalized scrub position for both actors. Page and gallery downloads save the current selection as the graph's default for reopening and export.

The laptop in `examples/wwzard-laptop.js` uses one projected deck plane for the keyboard, palm rest, trackpad and hinge. Its base and lid remain separate reusable scene actors. Open and Close interrupt the active action immediately, then leave the lid in the requested state. Wwzard continues through closed idle and a quiet pause until an open request; he does not reopen it automatically. Opening returns him to work, while visitor requests wait for the lid to reopen. The saved graph starts each wizard and screen clip together. The website's motion study previews both actors together for `close`, `closed-idle`, `closed-pause`, and `open`; Studio whole-scene playback evaluates the same saved graph. Editing one actor's clip in Studio previews that actor, so use whole-scene playback for their coordination. The older combined `laptop` clips remain in the source for compatibility but are not selected by the current graph or motion-study menu.

The lid uses intermediate 2D contour poses around its fixed hinge. The near sleeve's matching shapes and wrist tracks are authored against the same lid edge. The palm and sleeve remain behind the cover at depth 40. A small fingertip drawing at 60 appears during contact, while the lid rim at 70 covers its edge. This preserves the near corner without bringing the whole hand over the cover. These use the existing shared part-depth and opacity channels. Tests check that the closed lid matches the deck footprint and the hand stays within 1.1 scene pixels of the grip during the fold. This is authored contact for this asset, not a general solver for changed laptop dimensions.

Normal leans back with both hands supported on the closed lid and occasionally taps its fingertips. Its wrists remain planted throughout idle and pause. Closing settles directly into this supported pose without passing through the angry elbow pose or dropping and immediately raising the hands. Angry rests an elbow and forearm across the lid, shakes its head, drops its arms and looks up. Disappointed reaches for the opening edge, looks toward the window, sighs and withdraws without lifting the lid. Each returns to the same supported-hand pose, followed by a seeded 3.5 to 6.2 second pause before another attempt. An Open command starts immediately during both stillness and moving gestures. The last requested open/closed state wins if commands arrive during a lid movement.

The shared `hinge.z` channel lowers the flat lid rim beneath supported arms and restores its upright corner order before opening. The new poses and finger deformation live in `examples/wwzard-closed-laptop.js`; mood variants for the other actions live in `examples/wwzard-emotions.js`. These are ordinary editable clips and depth channels, with no emotion-specific renderer.

The near sleeve's depth split sits 10 local units from its shoulder pivot. The previous 29-unit split left part of the resting elbow behind the closed laptop, producing a straight cut. The corrected saved split keeps the shoulder behind furniture and the supported elbow continuous. SVG and Canvas regression checks compare the supported elbow against its complete contour throughout the Normal lean and return.

Angry's elbow-rest contour places the lower elbow inside the closed cover rather than across its near edge. Its sleeve and wrist tracks share the same authored contact pose. The contact check samples the full lower-elbow silhouette during the head-shaking hold, separately from Normal's support check.

Arm withdrawal uses independently keyed additive shape layers, `nearRetract.bend` and `farRetract.bend`. Halfway through the layer the hands have slid toward the body; the second half lowers them behind the desk. Wrist tracks follow the same displacement, and depth changes at the rear edge. The shoulder no longer swings the whole arm to clear furniture. These layers are editable in Studio's Shape target panel and survive project reopening and website export.

Normal rest lasts 1.8 seconds, Angry 1.6, and Disappointed 2.2. The slide-and-tuck takes 0.7 seconds; release from the angry elbow hold takes 0.85 seconds. Normal close is 3.9 seconds and open is 4 seconds. Completed greeting, curiosity and frustration actions check pending input directly before choosing rest. Open and Close use the shared action interruption contract: a 0.25-second blend from the displayed pose and evaluated contours while the incoming clip advances. Paired actions match the current hinge angle when reversing a moving lid, and canceled actions cannot fire completion effects. Repeated requests for the active action do not restart it. Studio Action recipes exposes the interruption policy, blend duration, and prop/channel matching; these survive save/reopen and website export. Visitor requests and mood-only changes still use authored action boundaries.

Wwzard finishes a typing phrase before noticing the visitor. The first response is a greeting. Later attention produces curiosity; several requests before a response produce frustration. A bounded pending flag combines requests, and memory records familiarity. Completed responses lead to a rest and a seeded pause before returning to work. Interrupted attention does not cancel an arm movement halfway through it.

The motion study on the page plays each movement at normal or quarter speed and permits frame scrubbing. Shared illustration-player `previewClip` and `clearPreview` methods preserve live scene time and behavior while showing a paused pose. Reduced-motion playback is still; explicit pose inspection remains available.

The typing diamonds follow the float/grow/pop/fade effect in the public [portfolio](https://jelizarovas.com). Four fixed drawing slots use different sizes, staggered births and different rise distances/lifetimes. Their opacity, movement and size morphs are ordinary saved `work` clip tracks. Other clips explicitly hide them, and each burst fades before the work action ends. They travel independently of the torso, retain scene depth, and require no host timers or particle allocation. Timing repeats with the authored typing phrase; it is not random on every cycle. Studio, character-library reuse and exports carry the same effect.

## Authoring and reuse

Studio exposes the rig, clips, timing and saved behavior rules. Save/reopen and website export retain the artwork, gradients, interactions, behavior, and mood variants. The character library can insert the same rig and clips into another scene; that insertion does not automatically copy the desk scene's behavior graph or props.

The Pose inspector's Scene depth section lets spatial rig parts inherit their actor's depth, use a fixed depth or follow a joint. Surface decorations inherit their host's depth. Furniture actors use the existing Scene inspector depth controls. Both sets of settings survive document transactions, undo, save/reopen and website export.

Each sleeve and hand now binds its fixed depth to its arm's animated `z` channel. During `rest`, the hands lift clear, switch to depth 2 behind the desk at 0.4 seconds, and lower out of sight. They lift again before restoring their normal order at 3.32 seconds. Hand opacity stays at 1. Studio's **Animate with joint** selector connects a fixed part depth to a keyframeable **Depth / z** channel. [Animated overlap order](scene-depth.md#animated-overlap-order) describes the shared contract.

Twelve character parts, four typing diamonds and five lid surfaces use the shared `spatial.morph` path interpolation. These are changes to the rendered contour coordinates, including Bezier control points, alongside the rig's translations and rotations:

- `torso.bend` compresses the robe and its shading between the collar and hem.
- `hatTip.bend` flexes four crown facets together. Their shared points remain aligned; the brim and buckle stay firm.
- `leftGrip.bend` reserves 0..0.25 for the near sleeve's ordinary bend and 0.4..1 for its laptop reach and fold poses. Existing actions copy their `leftArm.bend` timing into that first range; laptop wrist tracks follow the hinge drawing.
- `rightArm.bend` curves the far sleeve and cuff. `rightHand.y` follows the cuff's 10-unit lift.

Studio's Pose panel exposes the Shape / bend amount and keyframing. Selecting one of these joints also exposes its driven parts, a validated target-path text field, and intermediate contour frames as JSON. Edits use document transactions and survive undo, save/reopen and export. Every target must keep the base path's command sequence. Editing a sleeve's cuff travel requires adjusting its hand track too; this asset's correspondence is authored, not a general contact solver. Studio does not yet offer draggable Bezier points or curve handles.

Morph playback uses the original endpoint strings at amounts 0 and 1, caches control-point parsing and reuses held intermediate paths. No physics simulation or per-frame asset generation is required.

Authored gradients use `part.gradient` with normalized linear endpoints and two to eight ordered opaque color stops. SVG and Canvas use the shared paint contract. They require the `part-gradients` capability and take precedence over dynamic scene lighting. An actor's explicit appearance-channel color overrides its source gradient without deleting it. Dedicated visual gradient-stop controls and gradient SVG import are not included in this implementation; gradient parameters are currently scene data. Studio preserves them while editing the rig and performance.

Use **Download editable scene** on the page to save the complete source. Studio's website export uses the deployed runtime location; to produce a self-contained website directory, compile the downloaded JSON with `npm run compile:scene -- scene.json empty-output-directory` and serve the entire output over HTTP(S).

## Evidence and remaining limits

See [performance results](wwzard-performance.md) for size budgets, measured desktop frame delivery and device details. The checks cover behavior memory and recovery, deterministic replay, motion continuity, gradient rendering, save/reopen, compiled playback, second-scene character reuse, responsive layout, reduced motion, offscreen suspension and disposal.

Visual review found and corrected the initial redraw's proportions, hand position, keyboard perspective, hard return-pose snaps and far-arm occlusion. Browser playback checks cover separate closing, closed idle, closed pause, opening, and return to work in all three moods. Disappointed and Angry closed-idle clips completed quarter-speed Study playback. Sampled motion sheets are generated by `node tools/review-wwzard.mjs` against the local server. They are review evidence, not automatic aesthetic approval. `test/wwzard-deformation.test.js` holds all rigid transforms neutral and verifies that actual SVG path data changes, then checks contour continuity through clips and preservation in compiled export.

Physical-phone performance and sustained thermal behavior remain unverified. Visible settled illustrations still run the existing frame scheduler. This task does not establish event-driven sleep or complete the broader production-studio milestone.

`node test/wwzard-occlusion-browser.mjs` checks actual SVG and Canvas pixels across 132 poses covering all three moods. It verifies that the full robe cannot affect pixels covered by furniture, and checks palm/sleeve coverage inside the opaque lid panel plus supported-arm visibility above the closed lid. Moving the furniture exposes the lower robe, confirming that coverage follows the scene objects. The test supplements visual motion-sheet review; it does not establish arbitrary camera angles or automatic collision/contact solving.


Immediate-command verification covers all three moods at four closed-idle phases, repeated mid-fold reversals, exact input-frame pose/contour preservation, and movement on the next animation frame. Live buttons and complete interrupted openings were reviewed at embed size with a 390px desktop-browser viewport. Shared tests cover cancellation, replay, checkpoint restore, and release of captured paths after blending. Physical-phone latency remains unmeasured.
