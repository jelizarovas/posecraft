# Sprite puppets

Open **More options → Sprite puppets** in Studio. **Face controls** can hold the head angle, either eyelid or the mouth independently while the other tracks keep playing. Use **Face close-up** to see the drawings. **Key at playhead** records just the controls you moved. **Follow timeline** releases those preview overrides. The timeline also supports editing individual joint and drawing keys.

**Save puppet file** writes the rig, artwork and clip to portable JSON. **Open puppet file** reopens it. **Export website** embeds the document and player in one offline HTML file.

**Four on the Porch** has a 15-joint 3D skeleton and an orthographic sprite renderer. Select **Show joints on artwork** to inspect attachments or **Side view of joints** to see depth. The timeline accepts yaw and pitch keys in degrees. The head, wrist and pelvis can turn independently. The supporting foot holds a position in all three axes.

The 49 head views span 70 degrees. Three painted references supply the textures. Matched landmarks determine the depth of the face and the baker projects them at the intermediate angles. Eyelids and mouth each have 11 poses per angle. The waving hand has separate palm and dorsal paintings. Forearms, thighs, shins, boots and counting gestures use offline renders of rounded cross sections fitted to their silhouettes. The lot shares a 2560 × 3946 WebP atlas across performers, about 38.5 MiB decoded. The whistle is a mouth pose without audio.

Select **leftElbow · twist** to turn the waving hand on the left of the screen (the zombie’s right). From its palm-down stop at 0°, turn toward −180° to bring the thumb forward and open the palm. The counting forearm has the mirrored stop at +180°. The rotation axis passes through the elbow and wrist sockets of the assembled character. The wrist retains its own bend. Elbow flexion and knee flexion have separate hinge stops. Under **Joint limits**, change the minimum and maximum and choose **Apply limits** to update the track and its keys. The player interpolates signed angles inside this interval, including when the shorter route around the circle would cross a stop.

The rotation dial points to the selected joint on the artwork and skeleton. The green arc shows the allowed local angles. Drag **Preview rotation** or type a key value to try a pose. The pointer turns amber near a stop and coral at the stop. The readout names the stop and shows the angle. Scrub or play to return to the clip, or choose **Update key** to keep the edit.

Open **Collision shapes** and enable **Show collision shapes** to see the volumes attached to the pelvis, forearms and hands. Select a sphere to change its joint-local center or radius, then choose **Apply shape**. The side view shows depth. The waving hand passes behind the waistband as it lowers, while the counting arm passes in front. Studio, exported playback and the Adventures crowd use the same contact rules.

Each participating part stores its spheres as `[x, y, z, radius]` in units of part size relative to its socket. A moving part’s `space.collision.avoid` names the obstacle, the pivot and the intended side (`behind` or `front`). The solver pitches the arm about its shoulder, preserving distances between joints and the signed wrist twist. If an edited shape is too large to clear within the shoulder correction, the inspection view outlines the affected arm shapes in red.

The zombie uses a complete half turn for its greeting. In a study of 352 healthy adults, mean active pronation was 78–83° and supination was 85–88° across the reported sex and side groups. The zombie's angles are editable animation settings. Its hinge representation follows the same signed interval convention as the MuJoCo joint model. The head and facial artwork came from the original Adventures zombie toy reference. The ImageGen source sheets in `examples/characters/porch-zombie-art` provide a continuous waist-to-boot painting, a shoulder-to-fingertip painting, three shirt views and three counting-hand poses. A separate palm-facing drawing supplies the waving hand. The thumb points inward in the raised wave. Draw-order keys carry the entire far arm behind the waistband and trouser legs as it lowers. The far eye uses a separate, progressively occluded drawing during the turn. The baker samples overlapping pieces in each joint's coordinate system. Both thighs attach to the pelvis. Its waistband turns in depth and tilts during the weight transfer. Both thigh sockets compensate for the tilt so the supporting boot stays planted. The upper arms pivot inside the shoulder caps. Each view registers the upper-arm drawing and its pivot to the two ends of the painted cuff. Each sleeve follows its arm through weights painted across the shirt, with the collar and waist pinned. Editing a shoulder rotation key also changes the sleeve in playback and website exports. To reproduce the atlas and weights, run `python tools/bake-porch-face.py original.puppet.json output.puppet.json` with the original 16-cell document from Posecraft commit `35aac31`.

The runtime is exported from `posecraft/sprite-puppet`. Validate an imported document with `validateSpritePuppet`, call `compileSpritePuppet` once per bind pose, then use `sampleSpritePuppet` for joint centers, rotations and tile indices, or `drawSpritePuppet` for canvas playback. Parts are stored in parent-first order with rest centers in a common coordinate system. Each pivot is a signed distance along its local Y axis. The first matching base drawing variant wins. Each optional layer then samples its own drawing channel and the current view. Interpolated drawing channels round to the nearest registered cell. Existing step-keyed documents keep their original timing.

A part can bind its paint to child joints with a `skin`. Each drawing view stores joint pivots in tile pixels and a square grid of joint weights. A shoulder rotation changes the drawing about its registered socket. Players sharing an atlas reuse the resulting bitmaps. The cache holds up to 20 MiB of painted poses per atlas. The porch clip has 279 distinct bent-shirt poses at 60 Hz. Adventures prepaints them onto a shared 2048 × 2712 sheet (21.2 MiB decoded) so a fresh crowd can dance without filling the dynamic cache on its first loop. Other poses use weighted skinning.

Adventures' Porch binds this performance to assembled zombie decorations. Standard assemblies share a compiled rig and sampled poses and draw to one canvas per performer. Individually customized assemblies use separate painted layers. Both paths use the same drawing, joint and draw-order tracks. The performance document in `src/game/porch-zombie.puppet.json` is the same file that opens in Studio. To update the performance, replace that file with the reviewed Studio export. From Posecraft run `node tools/bake-sprite-skin.mjs ../play.littlelands.co/src/game/porch-zombie.puppet.json ../play.littlelands.co/src/game/porch-cloth`, then rebuild Adventures. Its offline build checks the puppet fingerprint and includes the sheet. The game's bind pose, placement and purchases remain in its own save.

For a hot-reloading seeded town, set `BENCH_ZOMBIES=8` and run `node --experimental-strip-types test/adventures-building-performance.mjs 960 --dev` in Posecraft. This starts Adventures Vite on port 5272 with an isolated local save.

Add `--navigation` to seed a second owned home and exercise touch swipes and three Star Map round trips. To measure the built game, use a width of 390, set `BENCH_HEADED=1` and omit `--dev`. The benchmark checks that the original artwork and animation clocks survive those trips, then drags a decoration for 14.5 seconds while all eight performers play.

For a fresh editor run, use `--first-editor` on the 390-pixel viewport with eight dancers. It records touch-to-visible latency, average FPS, worst frame interval and the number of advancing dance clocks.

Before the cuff and editor-preparation changes, foreground Edge 158 on Windows with Intel Iris Xe graphics ran the production lot at 390 × 844 CSS pixels and DPR 2. With eight 14-piece performers and 36 loose decorations, the 14.5-second drag averaged 60.0 FPS and its slowest frame took 17.7 ms. Yard and home swipes averaged 59.6 and 59.3 FPS. Three Star Map returns averaged 56.5–58.2 FPS, with one 66.7 ms frame. Opening the compact editor produced a 100.1 ms frame. All eight performers continued during subsequent appearance edits and rotation. These measurements used an emulated touch viewport on a desktop GPU.

Adventures 0.4.31 ran six fresh editor opens in the same eight-dancer scene on the desktop Edge touch viewport. Two opens held 60 FPS throughout. Four had one or two missed refreshes, with a worst interval of 33.4 ms. The editor entered view 91–107 ms after finger-down. All eight animation clocks advanced through every trial.

The pre-release 0.4.32 build before worker painting used the 3D joint limits and directional part sprites in that same lot. Two of six fresh editor opens ran at 60 FPS. Each of the other four missed one refresh and averaged 58.2 FPS. The longest interval was 33.4 ms and the drawer appeared 91–115 ms after touch-down. Each dancer painted at least 29 times per trial. Two 14.52-second drags averaged 60.0 FPS across 871 frames each, with a 16.8 ms 95th percentile and worst intervals of 17.2 and 17.6 ms. Changing a decoration’s appearance and rotating it each averaged 60.0 FPS while all eight danced. These results come from desktop Edge with touch emulation.
